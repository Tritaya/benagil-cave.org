Benagil boat tours: speedboat, small boat or catamaran?

Every boat that goes to Benagil uses the same western entrance and gets the same two minutes inside. What differs is everything around those two minutes: how long you spend getting there, how much of the grotto coast you see on the way, whether dolphins are on the menu, how wet you get, and what you pay. Two minutes inside: what every boat shares

Since August 2024 the Algar de Benagil is a regulated space. A motorised boat enters through the western entrance, holds inside for two minutes, and leaves; no boat lands on the sand, no one swims in. Since the July 2025 amendment up to five boats can be inside at once, and the skipper has to keep judging the conditions and abort the visit if it is not safe. So the cave visit itself is the same on a $13 speedboat and on a small private boat priced per boat. What you are actually choosing is the rest of the trip.

The rules every boat and every kayak share. Under Edital 019/2024 of the Capitania do Porto de Portimão, in force since August 2024 and amended by Edital 009/2025 of 2 July 2025: no landing on the beach inside, no swimming or floating in, no paddle-craft outing or rental without a guide. Motorised boats enter by the western entrance for two minutes, up to five at once. Kayaks and SUPs enter by the eastern entrance in groups of six with one first-aid-certified guide, up to fourteen craft inside at once, for up to eight minutes. Life jackets are compulsory for the whole visit. Full text, dates and sources on the rules page.

Which Boat?

The four ways to reach the cave, and who each one is for

Speedboat or RIB

Fastest and cheapest: 1–1.5 hours, a dozen grottoes at speed, from $13 out of Portimão (more from Albufeira and Lagos) with the biggest review bases on the coast. Expect spray, a hard bench and a quick two minutes in the cave. Pick this if you want the cave and the coast in one hour and don't mind getting wet. See the speedboats →

Small boat from Benagil, Carvoeiro or Armação de Pêra

Most cave time per euro: 15–25 minutes each way from the two towns, minutes from Benagil beach itself, so more of a 1–1.5 hour trip is spent in and around the grottoes. Calmer than a RIB, and small enough to go into the smaller caves too. Pick this if the cave itself is the point. See the small boats →

Catamaran or dolphin combo

Comfort and a second headline: 2.5–3 hours from Albufeira, Portimão or Lagos, some with a marine biologist, toilets and shade; the big catamarans view the cave from the entrance, and some send a smaller tender in, so ask. From $26–69. Pick this for families, seasickness, or dolphins as well as the cave. See the combos → or the caves and dolphins page.

Guided kayak or SUP

The only way to eight minutes inside, low to the water, through the eastern entrance, and the closest thing left to the old swim-in. Needs an early slot, a calm sea and a little fitness. Pick this if being inside the cave matters more than covering the coast. The kayak & SUP guide →

The trade-off: the speedboat is the cheapest seat on the coast and gets the same two minutes as every other boat, shared with the queue. The small boats from the nearest towns spend those two minutes too, then hover in the neighbouring caves for far longer. If you only get one morning, and the cave is the reason you came, leave from Carvoeiro or Armação.

Practical

Before you pick a date

  • The sea decides the day. The skipper judges the conditions at the cave and can skip or shorten the visit, and the Capitania can close the caves by a notice to mariners. No wave height is written into the rules. Check the sea conditions the evening before and prefer free-cancellation dates.
  • Light vs crowds. The skylight beam is steepest 10:00–13:00 from May to October; the first departures around 07:30–09:00 and the sunset runs give you a quieter cave and softer water. There is no legal time window inside the day, only a time limit inside the cave and no navigation between sunset and sunrise.
  • Where to park. Marinas at Portimão, Albufeira and Lagos have proper car parks; Carvoeiro and Armação have town lots. Benagil village itself gridlocks from July to September.
  • What to wear. Swimwear under quick-dry layers, a hat that stays on, strapped sandals, sunscreen, a dry bag for the phone. RIBs mean spray. The boat supplies the life jacket, which the rules make compulsory for the whole visit.
  • Private boats run from Portimão and Albufeira, priced per boat and several hundred dollars upwards, under the same cave rules; the bookable ones are in the private boats section.
