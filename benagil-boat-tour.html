Benagil boat tours: speedboat, small boat or catamaran?

Every boat that goes to Benagil stops at the same western arch for the same two minutes. What differs is everything around those two minutes: how long you spend getting there, how much of the grotto coast you see on the way, whether dolphins are on the menu, how wet you get, and what you pay. Two minutes at the arch: what every boat shares

Since August 2024 the Algar de Benagil is a regulated space. A motorised boat enters through the western arch, holds inside for about two minutes, and leaves; no boat lands on the sand, no one swims in. Boats under 12 metres can be inside three at a time; anything longer goes in alone or looks in from the entrance. So the cave visit itself is the same on a $14 speedboat and on a private hull priced per boat. What you are actually choosing is the rest of the trip.

The rules every boat and every kayak share (Edital 019/2024, Capitania do Porto de Portimão, in force since 13 August 2024, amended July 2025): no landing on the beach inside, no swimming or floating in, no unguided kayak or SUP rental into the caves area. Motorised boats enter by the western arch for two minutes, up to three boats under 12 m at once or one boat over 12 m. Paddle craft enter by the eastern entrance in guided groups of six per guide, up to seven inside at once, for up to eight minutes. Operators report the 2025 amendment raised the caps to five boats and fourteen paddle craft. Full detail and sources on the access-rules section.

Which Boat?

The four ways to reach the cave, and who each one is for

Speedboat or RIB

Fastest and cheapest: 1–1.5 hours, a dozen grottoes at speed, from $14 out of Portimão (more from Albufeira and Lagos) with the biggest review bases on the coast. Expect spray, a hard bench and a quick two minutes in the cave. Pick this if you want the cave and the coast in one hour and don't mind getting wet. See the speedboats →

Small boat from Benagil, Carvoeiro or Armação de Pêra

Most cave time per euro: 15–25 minutes each way from the two towns, minutes from Benagil beach itself, so more of a 1–1.5 hour trip is spent in and around the grottoes. Calmer than a RIB, small enough to be one of the three boats inside. Pick this if the cave itself is the point. See the small boats →

Catamaran or dolphin combo

Comfort and a second headline: 2.5–3 hours from Albufeira, Portimão or Lagos with a marine biologist, toilets and shade; the big catamarans view the cave from the entrance, and some send a smaller tender in, so ask. From $23–70. Pick this for families, seasickness, or dolphins as well as the cave. See the combos →

Guided kayak or SUP

The only way to eight minutes inside, low to the water, through the eastern entrance, and the closest thing left to the old swim-in. Needs an early slot, a calm sea and a little fitness. Pick this if being inside the cave matters more than covering the coast. The kayak & SUP guide →

The trade-off: the speedboat is the best value on the coast and gets the same two minutes as every other boat, shared with the queue. The small boats from the nearest towns spend those two minutes too, then hover in the neighbouring caves for far longer. If you only get one morning, and the cave is the reason you came, leave from Carvoeiro or Armação.

Practical

Before you pick a date

  • Swell decides everything. Over about 1.5 m the cave closes regardless of tide and operators cancel or reroute; November to March they run calm days only. Check the live reading the evening before and prefer free-cancellation dates.
  • Light vs crowds. The skylight beam is steepest 10:00–13:00 from May to October; the first departures around 07:30–09:00 and the sunset runs give you a near-empty cave and softer water. There is no legal time window, only a time limit inside.
  • Where to park. Marinas at Portimão, Albufeira and Lagos have proper car parks; Carvoeiro and Armação have town lots. Benagil village itself gridlocks from July to September, and the kayak, SUP and small-boat launches off Benagil beach tell you to arrive an hour early.
  • What to wear. Swimwear under quick-dry layers, a hat that stays on, strapped sandals, sunscreen, a dry bag for the phone. RIBs mean spray.
  • Private boats run from Portimão and Albufeira, priced per hull and several hundred dollars upwards, under the same cave rules; they have too few reviews for our cards, so use the live list at the end of this page.
